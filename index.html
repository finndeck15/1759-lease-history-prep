A place in time

One signature changed the course of brewing.

On the last day of 1759, Arthur Guinness secured the St. James's Gate site in Dublin. The brewery was already there; the lease offered something rarer—a stable foundation on which to build.

Over the centuries, the site grew from a local brewery into the home of a beer recognised around the world. The original terms became legend, but their practical promise was just as important: time, security and room to grow.

“The famous lease is more than a number. It is a statement of confidence in a place and its future.”St. James's Gate · Dublin

The St. James's Gate lease remains one of the defining chapters in the story of Guinness—and a reminder that the most enduring ventures often begin with a bold commitment to place.
